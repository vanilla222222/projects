What this is

Everything here runs in the page: the SMILES parser, ring perception, aromaticity, kekulization and hydrogen counting are implemented from scratch, and the descriptors follow their published definitions — Ertl's topological polar surface area, Wildman–Crippen log P and molar refractivity, Lipinski-style hydrogen-bond counts, and the strict rotatable-bond definition. The descriptor layer is checked against RDKit on a 40-molecule reference set; TPSA, log P, molar refractivity, rotatable bonds, the H-bond counts and the sp³ fraction all agree exactly. The ring and substructure counts, the Kier–Hall connectivity and shape indices and the remaining fifteen values are checked the same way across all 473 reference compounds below. Of the 7095 descriptor values that comparison covers, all but four match RDKit exactly, and both exceptions are known differences in ring and aromaticity models rather than arithmetic. Artemisinin's bridged trioxane cage has four independent rings by the cyclomatic count we use and six by RDKit's symmetrized smallest set of smallest rings, so its aliphatic-ring, saturated-ring and heterocycle counts read one lower here. Riboflavin's isoalloxazine ring system is aromatized by our perception and left partly non-aromatic by RDKit's, so RDKit reports two aliphatic rings where we report none — the same model difference that shows up on azulene. Both are reported rather than hidden by dropping the molecules.

The fingerprints are checked the same way. On the 473 reference compounds, every bit-count and all 111628 pairwise similarity scores match RDKit exactly for the atom-pair fingerprint. For ECFP4, 26 of those pairs differ and every one of them involves riboflavin, whose flavin ring system our aromaticity model treats differently from RDKit's; ECFP6 adds a single further pair. For FCFP4, 552 pairs differ, 472 of them riboflavin's, leaving 80 traced to two causes: 32-bit hash collisions inside RDKit's own fingerprint, where two chemically different environments land on the same key and are counted once instead of twice, and a tie that neither implementation can resolve the same way — when two atoms span exactly the same bonds, Morgan hashing keeps one environment and discards the other, and which one survives depends on hash values that differ between implementations. Removing the colliding keys from RDKit's intersection reproduces ours on the collision pairs, so the environment sets agree and the difference is in the hashing, not the chemistry. All of it is left reported rather than papered over.

The structural-alert panel is a hand-encoded set of 51 SMARTS patterns drawn from the Brenk unwanted-functionality list, the PAINS frequent-hitter motifs and the reactive groups screening decks remove as a matter of course. It is a subset chosen for coverage of distinct chemistry, not a transcription of the complete published catalogues, and the panel says so rather than implying otherwise. The SMARTS parser and substructure matcher behind it are written from scratch in this page and were checked against RDKit's own matcher: all 51 patterns across the 473 reference compounds, 24123 molecule–pattern comparisons, agree on both the match counts and the matched atoms, as do twenty harder probe patterns covering recursive queries, ring-membership and ring-size counts, valence, charge and bond-type alternations — 9460 further comparisons, of which one differs, artemisinin's fused-ring atom count under the ring-model difference described above.

The filters are the published ones (Lipinski, Ghose, Veber, Egan, Muegge, lead-likeness) and the bioavailability score follows Martin's tiers. Where a service like SwissADME uses a trained model that was never released — XLOGP3, its solubility models, its pharmacokinetic classifiers — this tool does not pretend to reproduce it; it uses an open method and says which one.
