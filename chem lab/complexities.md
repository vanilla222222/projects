| `js/naming-scaffolds.js` | 10 | The hardest-to-extend tier and the most novel code in the project, now also holding the project's first tetracyclic (cyclomatic-number-4) shape: the ergoline/lysergamide skeleton (LSD family), reached from `deriveName`'s `bonds.length === atomIds.length + 3` branch via `deriveTetracyclicName`. Unlike every prior fused shape here, the ergoline core is not a simple perimeter-with-hinge(s) topology — it has a genuine triple ring junction (one hub atom whose three ring bonds all lead to three other degree-3 fusion atoms simultaneously), so `extractErgolineCore` abandons the bond-removal/`orderRing`-perimeter approach used by every other extractor in this file and instead does a bespoke hub-and-walk classification: leaf-trim to a 16-atom/19-bond core, confirm exactly 6 degree-3 fusion atoms, find the unique "hub" fusion atom all of whose neighbors are themselves fusion atoms, walk outward from each of the hub's 3 neighbors in both directions until hitting another fusion atom (6 walks total), and classify the walks as "internal" (landing back on another hub-neighbor — 4 of these) versus "external" (landing outside the hub-neighbor set — 2 of these); the hub-neighbor hit by 2 internal walks is the indole-type nitrogen position (`n2`), and the walk chain lengths (2 vs 3) disambiguate the pyrrole ring from the benzo ring without any orientation-guessing step (unlike `carbolineOrientations`/`indoleOrientations` elsewhere in this file, which must try multiple candidates). The two external walks lead to ring D's far atoms, which must themselves be bonded to each other and then walked around a 4-atom ring C back to the amide-bearing carbon. `nameErgoline` then validates every element (all-carbon skeleton except the indole-type N and ring-C's N6), Kekulé-checks the pyrrole ring via `hasFuranPattern` and the benzo ring via `hasAlternatingOrders` (both reused as-is from `naming-ring.js`), checks explicit bond orders around rings C and D including the C9=C10 alkene, requires every skeletal atom to be fully "sealed" (no external substituents) except an optional N6-alkyl and a mandatory C8 carboxamide, and — since this carboxamide's symmetric-N,N-dialkyl substituent shape doesn't fit the shared `classifyAttachment`/`collectCoreAttachments` cascade — inlines its own `collectBranch`-based substituent walk rather than extending that shared cascade, deliberately keeping this new recognizer's blast radius contained to itself. Output is always a systematic `[6-<alkyl>-]N,N-di<alkyl>lysergamide`-style string (or `nor...` when N6 is unsubstituted), with `COMMON_NAMES` mapping only the one fully-confirmed case (`6-methyl-N,N-diethyllysergamide` → `LSD`) rather than guessing at ETH-LAD/PRO-LAD/BU-LAD structures. As with every other scaffold here, any topology that fails a gate falls back to the formula rather than being guessed at. Below this, the original bicyclic/tricyclic material is unchanged: `extractFusedCore`'s cyclomatic-number-2 fused-core recognition (exactly 9 core atoms and 10 core bonds, exactly two degree-3 fusion atoms with everything else at degree 2, exactly one shared bond, a single 9-atom perimeter once that bond is removed, then `arcBetween` splitting the perimeter into 3- and 4-interior-atom arcs), `indoleOrientations`' mirror handling, and `nameIndole`'s hard-coded Kekulé bond-pattern check and fixed indole locants. Also holds `matchEthylamineChain`, the one pattern matcher shared by both scaffolds, and `namePhenethylamine`, which is called from `nameForRing` in `naming-ring.js` — so this file and the ring file call into each other. Adding a second fused scaffold means re-deriving the arc-split and locant-assignment logic almost from scratch; there is no general ring-perception engine to lean on. Scaffold recognition must never fire on a near-miss: every guard here is deliberately strict, and a blocked match must fall back to the formula instead of dropping into the generic ring path. A second, independent fused-shape path now lives alongside indole: `extractNaphthaleneCore` recognizes the symmetric 6+6 all-carbon fusion (10 core atoms, 11 core bonds, two degree-3 fusion atoms, two 4-atom arcs), and `nameNaphthalene` checks both rings for Kekulé alternation, rejects any external substituent on a fusion carbon, and — since naphthalene has no heteroatom to anchor a numbering direction the way indole's nitrogen does — uses `naphthaleneLocantOrders` to brute-force the 4 symmetry-equivalent numbering orientations and picks the lexicographically lowest substituent-locant set, the same lowest-locants approach `chooseRingNumbering` uses for monocyclic rings. `deriveFusedName` dispatches to this path only after the indole path is exhausted; the two shapes are mutually exclusive by construction since `extractFusedCore` and `extractNaphthaleneCore` gate on core sizes 9 and 10 respectively. Quinoline-shaped input (a 6+6 fusion with a ring nitrogen in a non-fusion position) is deliberately rejected in this pass — `nameNaphthalene` requires every core atom to be carbon and falls back to the formula rather than guessing at a heterocyclic fused name. This file now also holds the project's first tricyclic (cyclomatic-number-3) shape, β-carboline (9H-pyrido[3,4-b]indole), reached from `deriveName`'s new `bonds.length === atomIds.length + 2` branch via `deriveTricyclicName`. `extractCarbolineCore` is purely topological, structurally distinguished from both bicyclic shapes by atom/bond count alone (13 core atoms, 15 core bonds, exactly four degree-3 fusion atoms with everything else at degree 2): it finds the 3 bonds induced among the four fusion atoms, brute-forces which 2 of those 3 are the true inter-ring shared bonds by checking which pair, once removed, leaves a single 13-atom `orderRing` cycle (the remaining, un-removed bond is the "hinge" — an ordinary ring bond that happens to join two fusion atoms), then walks that perimeter with `arcBetween` to measure the four gaps between consecutive fusion atoms and requires them to be a rotation of exactly `(0, 4, 1, 4)` — one 0-gap (the hinge pair), one 1-gap (the lone pyrrole-type heteroatom position) not cyclically adjacent to it, and two 4-gaps (the pyridine- and benzo-type arcs) on either side. That specific non-adjacency of the 0- and 1-gaps is the structural signature of this one linear fusion pattern; a different tricyclic topology with the same atom/bond count (an angular fusion, a differently sized ring, or a regioisomer like γ-carboline where the extra ring is fused to a different bond) produces a different gap rotation or fails the degree/bond-count gates outright and is deliberately rejected, falling back to the formula. Because the extraction never inspects elements, it cannot tell which of the two 4-gap arcs is chemically the pyridine side versus the benzo side, or which walk direction was taken — `carbolineOrientations` resolves this the same way `indoleOrientations` resolves indole's mirror ambiguity, except here it also takes exactly 2 candidates (verified empirically against a hand-built test graph, not assumed from the arc count alone), one for each 4-arc tried as the pyridine side (with the other arc and its hinge/fusion labels correspondingly reversed); `nameCarboline` then validates one candidate all the way through — exact `[C, N, C, C]` elements on the pyridine arc, all-carbon benzo arc, nitrogen at the lone gap position, all four fusion atoms carbon with zero external substituents, zero substituents on the ring nitrogen, and Kekulé correctness on all three subrings, reusing `cycleBondOrders` + `hasAlternatingOrders` for the two six-membered rings exactly as `nameNaphthalene` does, and reusing `hasFuranPattern` for the five-membered pyrrole-type ring since that ring's real bond-order pattern (both bonds flanking the ring nitrogen single, alternating double/single around the rest) is exactly the furan/pyrrole-type pattern already encoded there, not a plain alternating pattern — before collecting substituents at locants 1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 and 9 (the ring nitrogen at 9 can carry a substituent as a generic ring position, same as indole's N1) with `collectCoreAttachments` and assembling a prefix-only name ending in the literal `β-carboline` (the actual Unicode β character). This shape's 12-atom/14-bond trimmed core has four degree-3 fusion atoms whose only mutual bonds are the two real shared furan edges (no third hinge bond, since the two furans never touch each other the way carboline's pyridine and benzo rings both touch the shared pyrrole) — so after removing those two edges and ordering the 12-atom perimeter, the four gap lengths between consecutive fusion atoms come out as a repeating `(1, 3, 1, 3)` signature (the two 1-gaps are the free substitutable central-ring carbons; the two 3-gaps are each furan's own three non-fusion atoms) that fixes the topology outright, without any hinge search or orientation-list the way carboline needs one. `validateFuranArm` locates the oxygen freely among each 3-atom arc's three positions and checks the resulting 5-ring via `hasFuranPattern`, deliberately not hard-coding which polarity (single/double) the shared edge takes, since two rings fused on antipodal edges of one aromatic hexagon cannot both take the same fixed Kekulé polarity there — the central ring's own `hasAlternatingOrders` check is what actually constrains that. `nameDifuranScaffold` requires exactly one of the two free central positions (locants hard-coded 4 and 8, mirroring the real compounds' numbering, since the molecule's own topology doesn't otherwise privilege one over the other) to carry a `matchEthylamineChain` match, allows at most one ordinary substituent on the other via `collectCoreAttachments`, forbids any substituent on the furan-ring atoms themselves, and folds the amine into the substituent list the same way `nameBenzofuranScaffold` does, against a bare `benzodifuran` parent name. The actual cause turned out to be in `findPendantRingAtoms` (`naming-core.js`), not in the new saturated-ring functions: five of its pendant-branch resolvers (`resolveTriazolylMethylBranch`, `resolveSulfamoylMethylBranch`, `resolveAzacyclylMethylBranch`, `resolveOxazolidinonylMethylBranch`, `resolvePiperidin4ylBranch`) fire unconditionally for every single-order bond in the molecule, on the unstated assumption that the atom playing "core" (the `fromId` argument) is always attached to something bigger than the ring it's looking at. For a piperidine ring with one lone methyl substituent, that assumption is false: the methyl carbon *is* the entire rest of the molecule, yet `resolvePiperidin4ylBranch`, called with the methyl as `fromId` and the ring carbon opposite the nitrogen as `startId`, correctly recognizes a valid piperidin-4-yl attachment and — with nothing to stop it — strips the *entire six-membered ring* into the pendant set, leaving `deriveName` with only the bare methyl carbon as "core" and nothing ring-shaped to route to. This is the same general failure shape as session 9's biphenyl/COOH misfire (a resolver that only checks "is there a valid pattern over there" with no sense of which side ought to be treated as the core), just surfacing in a different resolver family. Fixed the same way in spirit as session 9's `isConfirmedRingAtom` guard, but simpler here since the actual discriminator is just "does the core-side atom have any other bond besides this one": added a `hasExternalAnchor = neighbors.length > 1` check in front of all five resolver calls, true only when the anchor atom has some other connection making it a genuine "rest of the molecule" rather than a bare leaf — which still allows the resolvers to fire correctly in their original intended use (e.g. a piperidin-4-yl reached through a `-CH2-` linker off a larger amine chain, where the linker carbon has two bonds) while blocking the degenerate single-substituent case. Verified with the same minimal repro (now returns `"4-methylpiperidine"`), the full 7-stamp test sweep (standalone and attached, all correct, zero valence violations), and the full 34-file scratchpad regression suite with zero regressions.
